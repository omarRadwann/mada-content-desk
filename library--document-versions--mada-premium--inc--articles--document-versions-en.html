When two files have similar names, a meeting can start with a discussion about the version instead of the actual question. A few clear labels can help without introducing a complicated system.

Use a name that explains the contents

Identify the document type, subject and version. A hypothetical example is “Financial information summary — version B”. Avoid repeated labels such as “final-new” or “latest-update” that do not explain the difference.

Status is more useful than “final”

Use labels your team understands: “in preparation”, “for internal review”, “internally approved” or “sent for review”. Internal approval does not mean government approval or confirmation of a zakat or tax treatment.

Give the file a clear owner

Identify who prepared the version, who can answer questions and who can approve it internally. One person may cover more than one role. The important thing is knowing whom to ask.

Before sending

Check the version’s status, explain the purpose to the recipient and agree the sharing channel and intended recipients. Do not place sensitive files in a public tool simply because sharing a link is convenient.

Use a system your team understands

An organised folder and a simple register may be enough. Start with a method the team can maintain and develop it as the volume grows. Organisation makes questions visible; it does not replace professional review of the contents.
